# Netty — a universe learning

A visual script for Mark William Miller’s film.

This tells the story in the language of the film. Times are approximate cues for the current animation, not limits on the eventual voice-over. This document is a readable script; it does not yet drive the player automatically.

## Throughout

The universe breathes.

Once born, the central twirl remains visible: the whitest white. Its two perpendicular rings contract and expand in counterphase.

Red and White share a center. They breathe in and out, exchanging which encloses the other. They never orbit one another. This is the pump, and it never stops.

Every structure has its own proportions, rhythm and history. Repeated forms belong to families; they are not interchangeable copies.

Let old forms recede as new ones emerge. Keep only enough of the past to show where the new form came from.

## 0:00 — Before particles

SHOW red and white flowing surfaces inside one another. No particles yet.

MOVE them with an irregular, searching wobble. One holds a little tighter than the other.

CHANGE: the wandering subsides. Both settle onto the same center. Their outlines smooth into two breathing surfaces.

## 0:10 — The breath brings White to life

FROM the settled breath, a white point wakes at the center.

FORM two perpendicular rings. One draws inward as the other expands. They spin and shrink into a small, brilliant twirl within the beating pair.

KEEP the red and white exchanging inside and outside.

SAY: “Four beats. Two pairs. Forty-two.”

A PAUSE, THEN: “Are we done? No. This is where we begin.”

## 0:36 — Each turn makes another sphere

SHOW a Black sphere appear with a completed turn of the twirl.

REPEAT: one rotation, one new sphere. Start slowly. Accelerate.

GROW their number, not their size, into an immense connected body with gaps between its regions.

MOVE the view toward one gap. The Black body fills the picture, then recedes completely as we enter.

## About 1:02 — Blue forms in the gaps

SHOW small Blue structures with nested spinning shells.

LET some bind and others repel. Strands join into asymmetric chains. Chains meet; their junctions become nodes.

OPEN the initially flatter strands into depth: a fine, breathing fabric.

FOLLOW one message along an arc. It reaches a node. The node changes. After a short response, new messages radiate along its other connections.

KEEP that receive–change–reply motion visible wherever the net appears again.

## 1:20 — Gathering, then release

DRAW Blue inward. Its fabric contracts toward the center.

RELEASE an expanding pulse across a briefly visible Black floor and through Blue.

FOLLOW the pulse outward. Matter spins up. The view opens into a cosmos.

LET the released energy gradually diminish. The pump continues.

## About 1:39 — Elements, then carbon

SHOW the elements arranging into a recognizable periodic table.

BRING carbon forward. Let the other entries recede.

OPEN four bonding directions around it. Make room for chains, relationships and the structures of life.

BEHIND this, galaxies turn around dark centers. Stars and planets form.

## About 1:44 — One world comes forward

FIND Earth in the cosmos.

BRING the rotating Earth toward us. Oceans, continents and atmosphere resolve.

SHRINK the galaxies into the distance as Earth grows.

CONTINUE inward. We are entering this world, not placing a new diagram beside it.

## 1:58 — Inside Green

REVEAL living cells: membranes, cytoplasm, organelles and nuclei.

CHOOSE one cell. Enter it. Its surroundings pass beyond the view.

APPROACH its nucleus. Find DNA at the center.

FORM the familiar double helix: exactly two green backbones, joined by clear rungs.

FOLLOW little Blue workers along the strands. They pause, receive, change a rung and continue. Leave the changes visible.

SAY: “DNA is an output language.”

A SMALL YELLOW ASIDE: “Unless you’re a yellow slime mold… Why settle for one nucleus? Bring thousands.”

## 2:12 — A bird has room to choose

LET the molecular view recede into living Green.

SHOW one bird moving among constraints: wind, gravity, an obstacle.

GIVE it room to turn, avoid, dart and choose a path.

SAY: “The Goldie zone.”

BRING in other birds. Highlight six neighbors around one bird, then let the links become faint.

LET the flock ripple and zip around its neighborhood. Keep it lively and irregular, not marching in formation and not drifting out of the picture.

## 2:26 — Life extends itself

RETURN toward a physical brain: Blue pulsing inside folded Green tissue made of Red material.

DRAPE energy around it. Show the tension between what is taken up and what remains in motion.

LET living intention become tools and extensions.

## 2:30 — Orange: our machines

BUILD ordered local grids around the living structure, in three dimensions.

GIVE the grids familiar cases and screens: tablets, phones, computers.

SHOW extension of human ability: a support, powered legs, a powered suit.

KEEP a human between the inner source and the outer computers. The person faces away from the center, toward the screens.

PASS messages outward and feedback inward.

SAY: “Our computers are becoming thoughtnets. The net came first.”

## About 3:00 — A thin human veil

FADE IN a faint older human likeness with a reaching gesture, recalling the Sistine Chapel.

MAKE it only a thin film around the working picture. Keep the internal Blue, Red and Green visible.

KEEP us there too, facing our machines. The central white twirl remains unmistakable.

## 3:08 — A learning surface

SHOW observation and response flowing both ways between people, computers and the inner net.

SAY: “We are the eyes of God.”

SAY: “All-learning, not all-knowing.”

NAME this hope: “The Age of Aquarius.”

END the words, not the activity. The net keeps responding. The pump keeps breathing.

## Further scenes to develop

These are captured intentions, not completed scenes in the current player:

- Patterns sent and received through a listening antenna: an attempt to establish communication.
- “Playing tic-tac-toe with God. That sounds expensive.”
- The hope of learning to focus large physical forces, including reducing earthquake damage, framed as a proposed future capability.
- Yellow living material organizing into more complex forms, as part of Mark’s origin story.

Keep these passages exploratory. Find pictures and pacing with Mark before assigning them fixed places in the film.

## What this teaches us about a shared movie format

Mark’s intended foundation is Talisman morphs: controllable points, stances, movement and change. The longer-term aim is a common scriptable movie format across Moondance games. Netty is research toward it; finishing the film takes priority over building a general system.

A readable action such as “Red expands around White, then they exchange” could bind to named morph controls and a repeating timing curve. “The bird banks toward its neighbors” could bind to a stance, orientation and a behavior. “Follow into the nucleus” could bind a camera to a named target and a transition.

Separate the readable direction from the exact bindings needed by a player. Preserve the underlying morph definition and motion so a fast preview and a detailed offline rendering can interpret the same performance. This is a design direction, not an implemented shared format or a claim that the current Canvas player already consumes Talisman morphs.
